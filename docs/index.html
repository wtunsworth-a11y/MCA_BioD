PILOT · STARTING ON THE MANAGALAS PLATEAU · PAPUA NEW GUINEA

Help put a name to the animals caught on camera in Papua New Guinea

Camera traps photograph animals that are shy, nocturnal and little studied. We're starting with images from the Managalas Plateau, and building a place where camera trap users across PNG, and in time the wider region, can give help, and get help, naming animals in their images.

Why we're doing this

Our aim is to support greater use of camera traps as a method for biodiversity surveys, and to develop a community of experts who can identify what they record. Naming difficult images together does both. We're starting with images from the Managalas Plateau.

More camera trap surveys

Camera traps are a powerful, low-impact way to survey wildlife, but every survey produces images that are hard to name. Making identification easier, and getting expert help, removes one of the main barriers to using them more widely.

A community of experts

Working on real images together builds identification skills and connects people who know these animals. The aim is a community of experts across Papua New Guinea, linked in time with Indonesia, Solomon Islands and other neighbours.

Hard images, open questions

These frames are the difficult ones: animals at night, half hidden, or from groups where species look alike. Some may be outside their known range, and some may be species not yet described.

Better tools for the region

Names that several people agree on become verified training labels. They help build automated tools that can sort camera trap images from New Guinea and neighbouring islands, whose animals are often missing from global datasets.

How to help

No special software or account is needed. The first time you submit, enter your email address and we'll send you a sign-in link. No password.

Choose your groupPick the animals you know best below: wallabies, bandicoots, cuscuses, monitor lizards and more.
Study the imageBrighten night shots, check the location, elevation and time of day, and compare with species known from PNG.
Give your best nameName it at the level you're sure of. Working names, notes and flags for range extensions or new species are welcome.

Choosing images to submit

For camera trap projects in PNG (and, in time, the wider region) that want to add images. We want a small, carefully chosen set from each project that the community can work through, not every frame from a survey.

Send selected images

  • Choose images that genuinely need a second opinion: animals you can't name with confidence after checking them yourself.
  • Send tens of images per group, not hundreds.
  • Send no more than 2 or 3 images of the same thing: the same species at the same survey location (cameras within the same 1 km square). Pick the clearest ones.

Group photos of one animal

  • If one camera activation (the same camera, within the same minute) caught an animal in several photos, send up to 3 as a set, chosen to show different features: head and ears, tail, feet, flank or markings.
  • Keep a set together by putting it in its own folder, or by giving the files the same name with a, b, c (e.g. G46_C2_0716a.jpg).
  • On this site a set appears as one sighting and is identified once, using all its photos. Photos minutes apart are kept separate, as there is no way to know they show the same individual.

Keep the originals

  • Send the original JPEG at full size, with the camera's data strip (date, time, station) intact. Don't crop, brighten or edit it.
  • Note the group (family) if you know it, and any doubts about the date if the camera's clock may have reset.
  • Tell us the project, country and province, and each station's coordinates. The site only ever shows a general location, never exact camera positions.
  • Leave out photos that show people, houses or gardens.

Previously identified images

  • Confirmed identifications are welcome as references: a few clear examples per species help people compare.
  • Send them in a separate folder named reference, with the confirmed name and who identified it, in the file name or in a short list.
  • References are shown as examples, not put to the vote.

Help this pilot grow

This is a pilot of the Managalas project, led by Landscape Alliance PNG and funded by the European Union. We are looking for partners to grow it into a national community of practice: camera trap projects across Papua New Guinea that want to share images, institutions and experts who want to take part, and funders who could help it last.

We would also like to hear from camera trap users in Indonesia, Solomon Islands and other neighbouring countries, where many of the same animal groups occur.

A pilot of the Managalas project, led by Landscape Alliance PNG and funded by the European Union, to grow a community of practice and identification expertise for camera trap surveys.

All images are from Ijivitari District, Oro (Northern) Province. A survey location is a fixed 1 km square; maps show only the 5 km area around it, never exact camera positions. Elevations come from SRTM data. Candidate species, names and elevation ranges come from the IUCN Red List of Threatened Species (API v4).

Identifications are stored in the project database, with a public copy of the agreed labels in the project repository. Email addresses are never shown.
